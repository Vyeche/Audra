# Design System: Audra

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Listening Room After Hours"**

Audra is a mixer seen in lamplight. The window is a dark, warm room: charcoal grounds with a faint plum cast, ivory type, champagne fader caps. One amber light falls on the mix being shaped. The output being edited and every strip that feeds it stand in a wash that fades down the strip. Everything else stays in the shade, still fully legible. The lamp moves when the submix changes (a 150ms cross-fade). Two more motions were added at the maintainer's request (2026-11-03, "fun animations when moving around"), each short and each meaning something: a row that opens grows its faders into the room (260ms, ease-out), and a toggle that lights flares like an LED warming up before it settles (220ms). Hover still brightens a control's fill and nothing else: no lift, no growth, no bounce.

The controls are objects (2026-11-03, the maintainer, holding up a hardware mixer's controls: "The nubs and sliders and buttons should look" like these). Knobs are turned caps with a pointer, faders are brushed-metal caps riding in a slot, keys are low blocks lit from above that light solid in their state hue, and meters are LED ladders. Audra takes that control language, not RME's slate skin, colours or artwork: the room, the lamp and the hues stay Audra's.

Zones divide by ground tone, not by boxes. The three mixer floors (inputs, playback, outputs), the top bar and the right-hand counter are full-width bands of slightly different darkness. Each strip is a brushed panel in a 1px hairline frame on its floor (the maintainer's choices, 2026-11-03: the frame, then "a subtle brushed look background"). The density follows the hardware mixer this replaces: 96px strips, a shared dB scale per row, and controls that sit where TotalMix users expect them. Type is never smaller than 12px, because the window is read in a dim room from arm's length.

The platform is iced 0.14 on Linux, not the web. Every colour role lives in one Rust struct, `Palette`, and the built-in theme is the constant `AUDRA`. Sizes are px at `ui_scale` 1.0, and the user can zoom the whole UI by hand between 0.75 and 1.75 (Ctrl+wheel, Ctrl+= / Ctrl+-, and the zoom bar at the foot of the counter).

**Key Characteristics:**
- Warm dark grounds, separated by tone rather than borders.
- One amber lamp wash marks the mix being edited and what feeds it.
- Coral marks only what can be pressed or is live.
- State hues carry meaning: green for signal, amber for attention and solo, red for clip, periwinkle for mute, ember for 48V.
- Three embedded faces: a serif for names, a hyperlegible sans for labels, its mono sibling for numbers.
- Hover changes fill only. Shadows belong to raised objects and to lit state.

## Colors

A warm charcoal-plum ground with ivory text, one coral accent for engagement and one amber light, plus a small set of state hues that each mean one thing.

### Primary
- **Rose-Gold Coral** (`accent`): the "engaged" colour. It is the fill of a lit Control Room key, an active view tab, and every lit toggle that has no state hue of its own (PAD, AN1→2, Cue, LOOP, low cut, EQ on, stereo link, group links). It is also the 1.5px edge of a selected strip, the lit LED of the selected snapshot or layout slot, the rim of every slot waiting to be stored into, the fill of a lit side key and the ring of one whose panel is open with its effect off, and the focus ring of a text input.
- **Plum Shade** (`accent_dim`): the quiet selected fill. Menu highlight, text selection, the ground behind a lit icon glyph.

### Secondary
- **Lamp Amber** (`lamp`): light. A lit strip's hairline frame takes it at 75% and the strip gives off a soft glow of it (14px at 50% on a dark theme, 10px at 70% on a light one; `theme::strip_glow`; critique #5, 2026-11-05: in Slate the wash alone was invisible), so the pool reads in every theme without touching the contrast of anything on the strip; selection's coral and the keyboard's glow come first. It is also the translucent wash on lit strips (alpha 0.16 at the head, 0.095 at 60%, 0.05 at the foot; softened from 0.19 on 2026-11-03, when the maintainer found the controls on a lit strip "hard to see with the black and brown"), the wash at the top of the counter (14% blended into `counter`, gone by 35% of its height), and, while Dim or Talkback is on, the level's note ("Dim: 20 dB quieter", "Talkback: you hear AN1"), Main's slider fill and the Main strip's DIM tag (the readout keeps the user's level: the Lamp Is Light rule). It shares its hex with `attention` but is a separate role, so a theme can split them.

### Tertiary (state hues)
- **Signal Green** (`signal`): meter signal up to −6 dB (as Quick setup's band says a good voice is; critique #58: from −12 a right level read amber in the Mixer), and "connected".
- **Honey Amber** (`attention`): solo, the simulated-device dot, soft warnings, and the meter zone from −6 to −3 dB.
- **Clip Red** (`clip`): meter zone above −3 dB, clip and errors.
- **Periwinkle** (`mute`): mute. It is the one cool note in the palette.
- **Ember** (`phantom`): a lit 48V, the colour of the unit's own phantom LED.

### Neutral
- **Top Bar Black** (`top_bar`): the darkest band, so the floors below read as lit.
- **Deep Well** (`bg_deep`): recessed tracks, the device chip, the EQ handles' discs. The meters' wells are not this: they are a fixed near-black in every theme (`fader::METER_WELL` #08080a), with their own fixed marks on it: the scale ticks `METER_TICK` #a8b2be, the unlit ladder `METER_UNLIT` #b88c88, the "no signal" dashes `METER_NO_SIGNAL` #3a3a3e (quiet on purpose, 1.8:1: a sign of nothing, not a reading) and the dark clip LED `CLIP_UNLIT` #3a1616. Words in a state's hue on `bg_deep` or on the EQ's screen go through `theme::readable_on` (critique #27).
- **Control Edge** (`control_edge`): the outline of a key at rest, the knob's value track (at 40%) and the rim of the fader's slot (at 75%). It holds at least 3:1 on every ground, the lamp wash included, so a button or a slot never melts into a lit strip.
- **Rest Lift** (`theme::key_rest`): the fill of a key at rest, `text_primary` at 18% at the top falling to 9% at the foot in a dark theme (a light theme's lift is a shade, so its top gets less: 5% to 13%); hovered, 25% to 15%; pressed, flat. It lifts whatever is under it, so on a lit strip it brightens the wash instead of punching a near-black hole in it.
- **Inputs Floor** (`floor_inputs`), **Playback Floor** (`floor_playback`), **Outputs Floor** (`floor_outputs`): the three mixer floors. Playback is the darkest of the three, so it separates from the floors on either side of it. The outputs tone continues to the foot of the window.
- **Counter Ground** (`counter`): the right-hand column, the lightest ground.
- **Surface** (`surface`) and **Surface Raised** (`surface_hi`): panels and flyouts, then the fill under the cursor or a key tile at rest.
- **Hairline** (`border`): one step above `surface`, so a panel reads as an object.
- **Warm Ivory** (`text_primary`): body text and values. It is also the placard's card colour.
- **Soft Ivory** (`text_sec`): labels, inactive controls, unlit icon glyphs.
- **Dusk** (`muted`): quiet text that must still read: −∞, empty slots, the dB scale numbers, guide words. It holds at least 6:1 on every floor, on the counter and on the lamp wash (raised from #b3a2ab, which fell to 4.8:1 on the wash).
- **Champagne** (`fader`): the metal of the fader caps (a light theme's is dark, so its caps are dark metal), the counter sliders' fill, and the border of a control under the cursor. It is also the keyboard's focus on a strip: a glow round it and its rim where selection's coral is not (text fields keep a coral rim while typed in: that focus is live, a strip's is a place). It is the brightest neutral in the chrome.
- **EQ screen** (`eq_plot::SCREEN` #0b0d0f, its words `SCREEN_INK` #b8bfc9): fixed in every theme, as a display is; the EQ graph's only colours outside the palette, with the bands' own hues. The D panel's graphs (`dyn_plot`: the curve and the trace) sit on the same screen.
- **Dynamics hues** (`dyn_plot`, `dynamics_panel`): the compressor red #e04a5a, the expander green #5cc86a (TotalMix's own pairing on its Dynamics knobs and curve), the turn-down light blue #6cc4f0 (its gain-reduction marks, on the D graphs and across a strip's meter top). On the screen they're drawn as they are, dashes on a dark edge so they read over the level; as words and knob captions on the panel they go through `readable_on(…, surface)`, grey while their part is off.
- **Ink Plum** (`on_active`): text on any lit fill (coral, ember, periwinkle, amber) and on the ivory placard.
- **Soft Ink Plum** (`on_placard_sec`): the placard's second lines.

### Named Rules
**The Coral Is Live Rule.** Coral appears only on something the user can press or something that is live right now (selected, engaged, a text field being typed in, open). Never use it for decoration, headings or static information.

**The Lamp Is Light Rule.** `lamp` is a translucent wash on lit strips and the counter, and the rim and glow a lit strip gives off. Its one opaque use is a temporary override on a level: Dim (its note, the slider's fill, and a **DIM** tag on the Main strip) and Talkback (its note on a Phones level). The level's number is always the user's own, as the fader shows it; the note says the effect (critique #39, the maintainer's pick: one rule). Both notes sit under the level's name, beside its number, in a few words with the sentence on hover ("Dim: 20 dB quieter", "Dim: coming back up" fading as it glides, "Talkback: you hear AN1", or "Talkback on" for a long mic name), on a line kept even when empty, so an override coming or going moves nothing below it; where the number draws small (a short window, zoomed), the note is one word on the name's own line ("Main 1/2 · Dim"), for the same reason. Dim never hides the placard's open-mic warning: it says "Open mic: AN1 · quieter while Dim is on". It never fills a button or colours any other text, with one sanctioned exception, the learning cards (`tricks.rs`, `celebrate.rs`): a find or a win is the lamp's moment, so their kicker ("New trick:"), a milestone's title and its words for the run take the lamp's amber (at `readable_on_floors`, so they read on every floor), and the milestone card's badge is an LED lens lit in the lamp's hue (the keys' material) with the lamp's light round it; a folded row's lamp dot, beside the words saying what it holds, is the lamp as light too.

**The Hue Is Information Rule.** Each state hue means one thing across the app: green signal, amber attention or solo, red clip, periwinkle mute, ember 48V. A lit state keeps its own hue rather than turning coral. The one sanctioned reuse is the EQ panel, where `clip`, `signal` and `mute` identify bands 1, 2 and 3 (`EQ_BAND`).

**The Tone Divides Rule.** Zones divide by a change in ground tone (`top_bar`, the three floors, `counter`), not by rules or boxes. The one frame is the strip's own panel and hairline, which mark a channel as a unit, not a zone.

### Materials
The controls' materials are derived from the roles, so every theme keeps them (`theme.rs`, "Materials"):
- **Key** (`key_rest`, `key_lit`, `key_lit_edge`): a low, flat block lit from above (`lit_from_above`, a top-to-bottom fill), with no shadow (distilled, 2026-11-03, from the maintainer's second set of references). At rest it is the Rest Lift; lit, its state hue solid like a lit lens (a lip 40% toward white across the top, 16% toward white just under it, 10% toward black at the foot; the maintainer: "Buttons are colorful when active"), with a rim 28% darker than the hue; pressed, it loses its lift. Keys can join into one block (`theme::Joint`: M|S, an output's M|CUE, the folded strip's M over S): the outer corners round, the inner ones are square, and the two rims meet in a seam.
- **Cap** (`cap_body`; `fader_metal` gives the fader caps the same silver on a light theme, `fader` on a dark one): a knob's body: dark anodised metal on a dark theme (`surface_hi` 11% toward the ink), brushed silver on a light one (`surface_hi` 20% toward the ink; dark caps sat heavy on a pale panel and made it busy, the maintainer, 2026-11-03).
- **Slot** (`slot`): shade over whatever is under it (black at 0.62, 0.55 in a light theme), the cut a fader cap rides in and the counter sliders' empty rail.
- **Panel** (`strip_panel`, `panel_lift`): a strip's brushed panel, a lift over the floor (the ink at up to 7.5% in a dark theme, the paper at up to 60% in a light one) shaped like satin down the strip: bright at the head, a soft band of light a fifth of the way down, falling away to the foot (`BRUSHED`); hovered, a little more. Under the lamp the sheen gives way to the lamp's own wash, which is a lift already (both together dropped a key's rim under 3:1 on Audra and quiet text under 4.5:1 on Nord). The contrast test checks text and edges on the panel, lit and unlit.
- **Well** (`key_well_round`, `WELL_PAD` 2px): the frame a joined set of keys sits in (the maintainer: "Some are insets"), shade over the panel (black 0.45 at the top to 0.28 at the foot; 0.16 to 0.07 in a light theme) with a darker rim, rounded to a capsule round the strip's M|S.
- **Inset** (`theme::inset`, `fader::draw_inset`; on a light theme flat black 0.10 with a crisp top wall at 0.30, rim 0.36 and a bright lip, so it reads as a cut, not a raised pill): a cut in a panel, a box within the box (the maintainer: "a closer interpretation of what a box feels like within a box and an inset and a bevel"); the lane, the meter's frame, the value boxes and the counter's meters and lanes are all one. Shade inside, deeper at its top (black 0.52 falling to 0.3; 0.26 to 0.17 in a light theme, still darker than its panel); a bevel as light falling from the top left, nested strokes three pixels deep since the canvas has no blur: the top wall in shadow (0.6; 0.4), the bottom wall lit, the side walls shaded left and lit right on a dark theme only (on a light panel shaded sides turned a tall lane into a tube, and a white inner wall read as a halo); a crisp dark rim (0.62; 0.36); and the panel's lit lip just under it. A strip fader's meter frame is drawn once into a cached layer under the live level.
- **Lane**: the Inset a fader's cap rides in (the maintainer: "The sliders look like actual sliders along a nice lane", then "It has padding and inset. Its has some margins to move up and down"): wider than the cap by 3px each side (26px), 6px rounded ends, standing 1px in from the fader's ends. The 3px slot runs down its middle, 6px short of its ends. Under the cursor its rim turns `fader`.
- **Ink on a control** (`ink_on`): whichever of `text_primary`, `surface` and `on_active` reads best on a control's own body, for the knob's pointer and value.

## Themes

Audra's own palette, above, is the reference. Nine more built-in themes share its every rule and role, with their own colours: **Daylight** (light grounds, dark ink; the placard turns into a dark card), **Graphite** (neutral grays), **Slate** (2026-11-03, the maintainer's hardware-mixer reference narrowed into a theme: cool slate grounds, light grey metal caps, a teal accent and a teal lamp, an orange M and a red 48V, an orange red kept 24° from its crimson clip so a lit 48V never reads as an over; solo keeps the amber it shares with attention), **High contrast** (black and white, yellow accent), **Midnight** (deep blue), **Crimson** (dark red, rose accent), **Monokai**, **Nord** and **Solarized**. They are picked from a grid of tiles, in the View page and on the Look page of Audra settings, each tile drawn in its own theme's ground, ink and accent.
- **Themes of your own** are JSON files (a built-in theme to start from and the roles it changes) with tiles beside the built-in ones. The one contract still applies: `theme::contrast_issues` checks each, and a theme that falls short gets an `attention` dot on its tile and says where in its tooltip, rather than being refused.
- **Change of theme is a change of light.** A theme picked by hand fades in over 450ms; with the theme by time of day on (Audra settings → Look → Appearance: a day theme and a night theme, each from a time), the scheduled change fades over 2.4s, smoothstep-eased both ways, every role blended (`theme::blend_palettes`). It is the one slow motion in the app, because it stands for dusk and dawn rather than a click.

- **One contract for all.** Every theme holds Audra's contrast rules, checked by a test: text 7:1 and secondary and quiet text 4.5:1 on every ground including the lamp wash, control edges 3:1, labels on lit fills (accent, 48V, mute, solo) and on the placard 4.5:1, panel text 7:1.
- **Roles, not colours.** Widgets read the roles through accessors (`theme::accent()`, `theme::text_sec()`, …) that follow the chosen palette; iced's own theme is built from it too, so no default text is left white on a light ground.
- **Meters stay meters.** The meter zones keep Audra's bright green, amber and red in every theme (`METER_SIGNAL`, `METER_ATTENTION`, `METER_CLIP`): a meter is read at a glance on its own dark well, as an LED ladder.
- **The wordmark follows.** Its letters take the theme's ink and its "A" the theme's accent.

## Typography

**Display / Headline Font:** Shippori Mincho Bold (embedded, Latin subset plus −, ∞ and ·; Bold is the only weight shipped)
**Body / Label Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (embedded at 400, 600 and 700)
**Numeric Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono (embedded at 400)

All three faces are SIL OFL 1.1, with their licences in `crates/app/assets/fonts/`. They are loaded as bytes in `main.rs`, so there is no system fallback stack. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is the app's default font.

**Character:** a brush-edged Japanese Mincho names things (the rows and the mix), and a sans drawn for low-vision legibility does all the work. The serif is rare, so it reads as a sign. The sans is everywhere, so it reads as the instrument.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (Shippori Mincho 700, 28px, `TEXT_BIG`): the placard's mix name, and the Main and Phones levels in the counter.
- **Headline** (Shippori Mincho 700, 17px, `TEXT_ROW`): row names ("Hardware inputs", "Software playback", "Hardware outputs", "Control Room").
- **Title** (Atkinson Next 700, 14px, `TEXT_MD`): strip names.
- **Title tab** (Atkinson Next 600, 14px, `TEXT_MD`): the top-bar view tabs and the level roles (Main, Phones).
- **Body** (Atkinson Next 400, 14px, `TEXT_MD`): default text, pick lists, top-bar labels.
- **Label** (Atkinson Next 400, 12px, `TEXT_SM`): the most common text in the app: toggle labels (M, S, 48V, LOOP), the placard's second lines, type tags, tooltips, hints, Undo/Redo.
- **Label strong** (Atkinson Next 600, 12px): the column's header bars and the device name.
- **Key** (Atkinson Next 600, 14px): Control Room keys only, in sentence case ("Dim", "Talkback", "Speaker B"; 2026-11-03, the maintainer's reference, replacing 12px bold capitals). The key's name stays upper case inside Audra (`control_room::key_words` shows it), where it also names the key's motion.
- **Numeric** (Atkinson Mono 400, 13px, `TEXT_NUM`): every changing number on a strip (dB readouts, gain). It is one step above the label floor, so values read first.
- **Numeric small** (Atkinson Mono 400, 12px): the dB scale numbers and the guide words.

`TEXT_SM` is the 12px floor. The older `TEXT_MICRO` and `TEXT_XS` names were merged into it. `TEXT_XL` (22px, Atkinson Next) is reserved for rare large titles ("No interface found"), the arrow connector in the routing views, and the name when the drawn wordmark cannot load.

### Named Rules
**The 12px Floor Rule.** No text in the app is set smaller than 12px at scale 1.

**The Serif Is a Sign Rule.** Shippori Mincho sets only row names, the placard's name, the counter's two monitor levels, the heads of pages, panels and About's lists, and the titles of the learning cards (a guide's at 14px, a milestone's and a guide's done title at 17px). Never use it for labels, buttons or body text.

**The Fixed Slot Rule.** A number that changes holds its width. Strip numbers use the Mono. The 28px Main and Phones levels use the serif's figures, each centred in a fixed cell (digits 0.66em, point 0.28em, minus 0.79em), with ∞ drawn from the Mono because the serif lacks it. The serif is used there because both Atkinson faces slash their zero, and at 28px that zero read as the phase symbol Ø.

## Layout

## Elevation & Depth

Audra is tonal first. Depth comes from ground tone (darker means further back: top bar, then playback, inputs, outputs, counter) and from light (the lamp wash and the counter's wash). The main surfaces are flat and carry no shadow.

There are four kinds of shadow, and each one has a reason:

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Placard** (black at 0.6, offset 0 8px, blur 22px): the ivory Now Mixing card stands on the counter like a printed card. It is a plain shadow with no coloured glow. Where the column draws snug or tight (a short window, 125%), it stands close: black at 0.45, offset 0 3px, blur 8px (`theme::placard_close`), so the shadow ends above the levels' first line (critique #40: under the full shadow "· Dim" read 3.31:1 on Daylight; now 5.36:1).
- **Dialog** (black at 0.55, offset 0 10px, blur 30px): floating dialogs such as About and the settings modals. The notice at the window's foot carries a × and stays while the pointer is on it; an error (a refused device write) stays until closed, others go after 5 s. A notice about a change Undo takes back carries an **Undo** capsule key before the × (`say_undoable`; critique #40, the maintainer's pick), shown only while that change is the last undo step (`push_undo`, Undo and Redo drop it) and the mix is unlocked; such a notice stays up while its key does (the maintainer's pick after critique #41). The key is 24px tall with `SPACE_LG` either side of its word, and its edge is 1.5px in a colour that reads at 3:1 on the notice wherever it lands on the pixel grid (`theme::notice_key`, `split_safe_edge`; critique #41: a 1px rim between two pixels read 2.16:1). Its tooltip says what it puts back ("Turn PAD back on (Ctrl+Z)"), so the words don't say "Undo" again; they stay to two lines beside the key. A saved take's note carries an **Open folder** key instead (`NoticeKey::OpenFolder`; the maintainer, 2026-11-13), under the words, since that note runs to four lines (critique #46: beside them it squeezed the note to seven), its × pinned at the top right. The rule: a key that fits beside two lines of words stands beside them; a long note puts its key under them. The note leads with the take's length and folder in words ("Saved a 0:09 take in your Music folder, under Audra."), and a take with dropouts (`recorder::Gaps`: the sound stopping dead and staying at exactly zero for 5 ms to half a second) says so and what to do, and stays until closed. A notice never re-lays the column's head: snug and tight are decided from the learning card in the slot, shown or under the notice (`sidebar::card_slot_h`). The first close of the window where a tray keeps Audra running asks once, "Keep Audra running in the tray?", a capsule key to keep running and a ghost one to quit, the answer kept (2026-11-05, the audit: closing hid Audra without a word). About's keys and gestures sit under a serif "Keys and gestures" head in four groups (Views and panels, The strip you're on, Mixes and snapshots, Mouse; group names in 12px Atkinson Semibold, `text_primary`), in a scrolling area, and the card keeps `SPACE_XL` from the window's edges (2026-11-05, the critique: 27 rows in one list ran off the window).
- **Raised control** (a knob or fader cap: its own drawn shadow, a tight dark one under its edge and a soft one a little below): the turned and machined parts are objects standing on the strip. Keys are flat blocks and cast none.
- **Lit state** (the state's own hue at 0.45, offset 0, blur 6px; 0.25 and 3px while pressed): a lit M, S or 48V toggle glows like an LED. This is a state, never a hover effect. **Bloom:** the moment a toggle or a Control Room key lights, its glow flares (blur up to 18px, alpha up to 0.85, the fill lifted 30% toward white) and settles to the lit state over 220ms (`motion::BLOOM`).

### Motion
Motion explains a change; nothing runs continuously. These motions in all:
- **The lamp** cross-fades to the new mix (150ms).
- **A row opening** (one row at a time) grows its faders from 35% to full length over 260ms, quartic ease-out. The row that closes folds at once: exits are quicker than entrances.
- **A lit toggle's bloom** (above), 220ms.
- **Store's sweep:** after Store, the slots' coral edges light one after another in reading order (each over 140ms, the next 28ms later), then hold: TotalMix flashes the slots, Audra lights them once.
- **A theme's fade** (above, Themes).

The maintainer asked for more (2026-11-03: "Ui should give more feedback to the user", "The animation should be subtle and fun. The sounds should guide the user to what they need. The ui should be intuitive"), so the controls answer the hand:
- **A glide** (`widgets::feel::Glide`): a fader or knob whose value jumps from elsewhere (a snapshot, undo, a reset, a double-click) travels there like a motorised fader, 260ms, quartic ease-out. That is the cap; the sound has its own safety: a click that sets a level more than 6 dB higher (a double-click's reset, a press on the lane off the cap, the counter's sliders, the Matrix's double-click and Ctrl+click) glides the level itself up over 300ms (`app::set_level_gently`, `fader_glides`), as a mic preset's gain does, and a hand on the fader, Undo or a snapshot stops it; a double-click never turns up the Main, Main B or the Phones, and a notice says so (2026-11-05, the critique: a double-click on a Main at −30 dB put 0 dB in the speakers at once). A double-click resets a fader or a Matrix cell to 0 dB (BF §21.3; the Matrix had used −2.5 dB). A small step (under 4% of the travel: a group or a remote surface following a drag) follows in 50ms, so followers never trail the hand. Letting go of a Solo, a PFL or Cue from its key (or Live Mode's switch) eases back what it silenced over `EASE_FOR`, as Dim does (`control_room::ease_solo_release`, `ease_cue_release`; issue #37); a snapshot, Undo or quit lands at once. A fader-group member that would rise more than 6 dB in one step glides there (`GlideVia::Member`, its Trim Gains mixes by each step; issue #38).
- **A splash** (`feel::splash`, 420ms): a control landing on its home sends a coral (or the knob's tint) ring out from itself, fading: a fader reaching or passing its default level and a centred knob (pan, an EQ band's gain) passing its centre, as the detent ticks, and both on a double-click reset. A knob's whole track flashes as it leaves.
- **A motor's landing** (`feel::spring`, 300ms; the maintainer's fun pass, 2026-11-06, then critique #11: a hand's release bouncing was under 2px and not hardware-true, a hardware fader stops dead under the hand): a cap driven to a new level (a recall, Undo, a reset, a mix picked: every fader drives to its level in the new mix, as a motorised console does switching layers) glides there and runs on past its mark as it arrives, 5px at its peak, swinging back twice, its spring starting 60% through the glide so it reads as one overshoot, not a stop and a twitch; a later follow step keeps it to the glide's new end. Never under the hand: not while the wheel turns the fader, not on a group's faders while a fader is held (`widgets::hand_on_fader`). The picture only: the level reaches the unit exactly when moved. The detent (`SNAP_PX`, `snap_width`): a hand passing 0 dB is caught there however fast it moves and held until it has travelled 5px on, giving 2.5px at its peak as it's caught; a hand approaching home is held within half a dB of it at most (critique #11: on a 120px fader 5px swallowed −3 to +2.6 dB); Shift (fine) goes through quietly.

### Named Rules
**The Hover Is Fill Rule.** Under the cursor a control only brightens its fill, or its border shifts to `fader`. Nothing lifts, grows or glows on hover, with one exception: a control that holds another points at it, so the pointer on Mute FX rings the FX key it holds and the pointer on that strip rings Mute FX (`theme::link_ring`); the ring is on the other control, never the one under the cursor. A lit strip keeps its wash under the cursor. "Brightens" follows the theme (2026-11-03, the maintainer: "Brighter or darker depending on the theme"): toward white in a dark theme, toward black in a light one (`theme::hover_toward`), so the control stands further off its ground either way. A knob's cap moves 14% that way and its track comes up; a fader's metal moves 14% (25% held) and its lane's rim turns `fader`; a key lifts and its rim turns `fader`. Checked in all nine themes.

**The Press Goes Down Rule.** A pressed control darkens its fill by 30% toward black (`PRESS_DARKEN`), so a click reads as a press rather than a hover held longer.

## Shapes

Corners are small and practical, on a three-step scale, with one in-between step for the strip's foot.

- `RADIUS_BOX` (4px): the strip's value box (the narrow switch's slot has a half-round inner end).
- `RADIUS_XS` (3px): the strip's keys (toggles; a joined pair rounds only its outer corners), strip icon buttons, the level slider's rail, crosspoint cells. Fader caps and the counter sliders' handles round at 2px.
- `RADIUS_SM` (6px): the big keys (Control Room keys, slots), view tabs, inputs, pick lists, menus and scrollers, plus the placard and the strip's frame and selection edge.
- `RADIUS_MD` (10px): panels, which are flyouts, tooltips and dialogs, and the device chip.

Borders are 1px hairlines in `border`, shifting to `fader` under the cursor. Floors have no border; a strip has its hairline frame. The only drawn icons are canvas strokes (1.3px, round knobs), such as the wrench (a strip's Settings), the gear (Audra's), the unit (the device's), the narrow key's solid triangle and the panel toggle. The UI faces have no icon glyphs, so an icon is drawn rather than set as text.

With the plain words on the card speaks them ("computer audio", "your headphones"); the spare output keeps its code, which the recording app lists. The placard's Fed by names a playback "computer audio" once, the input row's head says "sending to your speakers", and the Matrix heads take a 12 px `text_sec` second line of `mix_role_short`. Recall glides as a snapshot's does and is one Undo. Replacing it asks first, as Store does: "Save the mix now as My setup…" turns into "Replace My setup with the mix now?" with Replace and Cancel; Esc or leaving Quick setup cancels it.
- **Page keys** (`theme::page_key`): the small capsule keys these features add (Done, Help's What's this, My setup's keep / save / Replace / Cancel) take a 1.5 px rim pulled toward the ink until a 75% covered pixel reads 3:1 on every page ground (the floors, `surface`, the banner tint): a 1 px rim on a half pixel read 1.7-2.6.
- **Plain words** (`app::plain_role`, `plain_tip`, `input_words`): at every window height, in the band an input keeps for its gain row (`StripParams::mic_line`, so the row is as tall as the fader budget counts and no fader shortens), a line under an output's name (your speakers, your headphones, optical 1/2…) and a playback's (to speakers, to headphones, else computer audio), 12 px `text_sec`. Inputs keep their row's height: their kind is in the name's hover ("optical in (S/PDIF)", "mic or line in (XLR)"). A switch in Audra settings turns them off. The optical inputs (no 48V, no gain) take theirs in that band too ("optical in 1/2" … "optical in 7/8"), named as the outputs below; a folded row's head speaks them ("computer audio feeds your speakers"). What's this's banner (30 px) comes out of the faders while it shows (`EXPLAIN_BANNER_H`), so the page never scrolls for it. One voice per page (critique #84 A): on Quick setup the Phones are "your headphones" on the tiles, the card, step 3 ("your headphones (Phones 3/4)") and the placard's "listens on" line; the card names the mic "Your mic (AN1)" then "the mic", and says "Your speakers don't play the mic" (never "out of your speakers", which reads as playing from them); no "loopback" on a card. ## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take every colour from the `Palette` struct. A new theme is a new `Palette` value that replaces `AUDRA`, and widgets must not hard-code a hex.
- **Do** divide new zones by a step in ground tone, as the floors, top bar and counter do.
- **Do** keep coral for what is pressable or live, and give each new state its own existing hue (signal, attention, clip, mute, phantom) rather than coral.
- **Do** set every changing number in a fixed-width slot: Atkinson Mono at 13px on strips, fixed serif cells at 28px in the counter.
- **Do** use the named tiers (`TEXT_*`, `SPACE_*`, `RADIUS_*`) and multiply them by `ui_scale`.
- **Do** draw icons as canvas strokes in the style of `strip_icons.rs` and `panel_icon.rs`.
- **Do** make hover a fill change and press a 30% darkening.

### Don't:
- **Don't** set text below 12px at scale 1.
- **Don't** draw rules between floors or boxes round zones (a strip's own panel and hairline are the one exception).
- **Don't** use `lamp` as a button fill, or as a fill or text colour outside a level's override (Dim, Talkback) and the learning cards.
- **Don't** use Shippori Mincho for labels, buttons or body text.
- **Don't** lift, grow or glow a control on hover. Glow belongs only to a lit state.
- **Don't** make a sound for hover or a drag's every step: sounds mark a press, a home reached, something kept, refused or disarmed.
- **Don't** take an icon from a font glyph (a fallback "⚙" arrives at the wrong weight and size from whatever system font answers).

### Extending the system
Every colour role is a field of `theme::Palette`, and the built-in theme is `theme::AUDRA`. User themes (planned in PLAN.md) replace `AUDRA` with another `Palette`. The widgets read the roles, so they need no change. One caveat: `ON_PLACARD_SEC` is still a standalone constant outside `Palette`, so it must move into the struct before themes ship. A theme should keep the role contracts: `on_active` must hold AA (ideally 7:1) on every lit fill, `muted` must hold 4.5:1 on every floor and on the counter, and the three floors must stay distinguishable from one another.
